    PSIOPS Field Manual — Content Edit Report¶

    Scope: accuracy, clarity, and consistency of mechanical information across the live site (joemoose.github.io/psiops). Author voice and intent preserved throughout — suggestions are mechanical/editorial only.

    0. Master finding: the manual is two un-merged generations¶

    The eight pages fall into two sets that were clearly built at different times and never reconciled:

    Almost every cross-file contradiction below is a symptom of this split. The newer set (Appendix Rules Summary, Equipment, Zones) is consistently the more developed system. Recommended approach: treat the newer set as canonical, then back-port its rules and terminology into the four older chapters — rather than fixing contradictions one at a time. Two things to verify first:

    1. Stale build / partial deploy. The four older chapters serving a different site_name, repo_url, and logo strongly suggests they were last rendered under a previous mkdocs.yml and not regenerated in your latest deploy. A clean mkdocs build --clean + redeploy may already resolve the chrome differences (title, logo, nav, edit-link). Confirm against source before treating these as content bugs.
    2. The engigma edit-link is broken. On the four older pages the "Edit this page" link points to github.com/joemoose/engigma/... (a typo of enigma), which won't resolve to your psiops repo. Fixed by the rebuild above.

    1. High-priority mechanical contradictions (must reconcile)¶

    These are cases where the same rule is defined two incompatible ways. A GM using the body text and a GM using the Appendix summary will run different games.

    1.1 Psionics: two-tier talent vs four-tier neural weapon¶

    • Where: Character → Psionic Talents vs Appendix → Deploying Neural Weapons.
    • Problem: Character defines a two-tier roll (+WILLPOWER; 6– fails and locks out until rest, 7+ manifests; no cost). The Appendix defines a four-tier roll with a fatigue cost (6– no manifest +1 fatigue & lockout; 7–9 manifest +1 fatigue; 10–12 manifest; 13+ greater effect). They are different resolution systems, use different names (psionic talent vs neural weapon / neural power), and disagree on whether using a power costs fatigue.
    • Suggested fix: Adopt the Appendix four-tier + fatigue version as canonical. Rewrite the Character intro paragraph to match, e.g.:

      "Each time you use a psionic talent, roll 2D6 + WILLPOWER. On a 6 or less the power fails and you take 1 fatigue; you can't use that talent again until you rest at a rendezvous point. On a 7–9 it manifests but you take 1 fatigue. On a 10–12 it manifests cleanly. On a 13 or higher it manifests with a greater effect — work with the GM."

    • Also decide terminology: psionic talent (Character, used throughout the talent table) vs neural weapon (Appendix, Equipment armor warning). Pick one. If neural weapon is the in-world institutional term and psionic talent the descriptive one, say so once and use them deliberately; right now they read as an unfinished rename.

    1.2 The death / "wounds at 0 HP" rule¶

    • Where: Combat → Damage, Injuries, and Dying vs Appendix → Wounds — 0 HP.
    • Problem: Fundamentally different.
    • Combat: three tiers (6– / 7–12 / 13+). Non-fatal results give 1 injury + 1 stress; a separate +STRENGTH roll determines unconsciousness; 13+ rallies to lowest D6 HP.
    • Appendix: four tiers (6– / 7–9 / 10–12 / 13+). 7–9 gives 2 injuries, 2 stress, 2 fatigue; 10–12 gives 1 injury, 1 stress, 1 fatigue and is auto-unconscious; 13+ rallies 1D6 HP. Adds a helmet exception (knocked down, not unconscious).
    • Suggested fix: Adopt the Appendix four-tier version (it integrates fatigue, the helmet rule from Equipment, and a cleaner tier structure). Replace the Combat Damage, Injuries, and Dying outcome list wholesale with the Appendix wording, then confirm:
    • "rally with 1D6 HP" (Appendix) vs "HP equal to the lowest D6" (Combat) — pick one.
    • "up to your current maximum" (Appendix) vs "up to your maximum HP" (Combat) — current is correct given injuries lower the max; use it.
    • Term: Combat calls it injury / "Damage, Injuries, and Dying"; Appendix and Equipment call it wound ("Wounds — 0 HP", helmet "when wounded"). Standardize on one (recommend injury, since the talent Recover and the Injuries subsection already use it), or the Equipment cross-link to #wounds stays broken.

    1.3 Stress relief amounts¶

    • Where: Stress → Reaching Maximum Stress vs Appendix → Stress — Reaching 6 Points.
    • Problem: Different numbers.
    • Stress: 7–9 removes 1, 10–12 removes 2.
    • Appendix: 7–9 removes 0 ("keep it together for now"), 10–12 removes 1.
    • Suggested fix: Choose one schedule and make both tables identical. (No mechanical reason to prefer either; the Appendix is stingier. Decide and propagate.)

    1.4 Panic & Transcendent tables — renamed and remapped¶

    • Where: Stress → Panic Table / Transcendent Table vs Appendix → Panicking / Locked-In.
    • Problem: Same six effects in each, but renamed and re-ordered, so the two tables produce different results for the same D6.
    • Panic: Fearful→Afraid, Frail→Weak, Reckless→Shutdown; the −1 armor result is #2 in Stress but #6 in Appendix, "fail initiative" is #3 vs #2, etc.
    • Positive: Transcendent → Locked-In; Dexterous→Sharp, Powerful→Jacked, Stalwart→Hardened, Courageous→Steady; rows remapped.
    • Suggested fix: Pick one table per outcome and delete the other version. Make the D6→effect mapping identical in both the body chapter and the Appendix. Also unify the table names (Panic vs Panicking; Transcendent vs Locked-In) and the trigger verb in the "courageous/steady" entry — see 2.x on success vs complete success.

    1.5 Spending momentum — costs and option names disagree¶

    • Where: Operations → Spending Momentum vs Appendix → Spend Momentum (and Stress → Reducing Stress).
    • Problem:
    • The resist-a-consequence option is DETERMINATION ROLL, cost 3 (Operations) but EVASION, cost 4 (Appendix). Different name and cost.
    • FLASHBACK costs 4 (Operations) but 6 (Appendix).
    • "Spend 1 momentum to remove 1 stress" is a real spend option (Stress chapter) but appears in neither momentum menu.
    • Suggested fix: Build one canonical momentum-spend table and use it verbatim in both places, including the stress-removal line. Recommended consolidated set (reconcile costs to your intent):

      ASSIST (1) · CLEAR STRESS (1, remove 1 stress) · PUSH YOURSELF (2) · RESIST CONSEQUENCE (3–4, the determination/evasion roll) · FLASHBACK (4–6)

    1.6 Initiative — pass/fail vs four-tier¶

    • Where: Combat → Initiative vs Appendix → Initiative roll.
    • Problem: Combat treats it as binary (+TACTICS; fail = lose your turn) and never states the success number. The Appendix gives a four-tier table where 7–9 lets you act first but with disadvantage on your next action roll, and 13+ grants advantage. The body text has neither the partial-success downside nor a stated threshold.
    • Suggested fix: Replace the Combat Initiative outcome with the Appendix four-tier table (it resolves the missing threshold too). Then reconcile with Surprise: state explicitly whether a surprise "free round" happens before initiative is rolled or replaces the first-round initiative check.

    1.7 Retreat — pass/fail vs four-tier¶

    • Where: Combat → Retreat (and Attacks of opportunity) vs Appendix → Retreating.
    • Problem: Combat says retreat "always requires a successful roll +REFLEXES"; the separate Attacks of opportunity rule says disengaging from close range provokes a free melee attack unless you take an action + REFLEXES roll. The Appendix gives a four-tier Retreating table (6– provokes a free attack; 13+ you attack then disengage). The body text and summary describe the same situation with different machinery.
    • Suggested fix: Adopt the Appendix four-tier Retreating table as the resolution, and rewrite Combat → Attacks of opportunity to point to that table rather than restating a second, simpler rule.

    1.8 The range / movement system is defined twice and incompatibly¶

    • Where: Combat → Ranges + Weapon Range Modifiers vs Zones (whole chapter).
    • Problem:
    • Combat uses meters: "when a character spends an action moving on foot, they move 10 meters," Close = 2 m, etc. Zones explicitly rejects measurement: "Ranges become relationships between zones, not measurements… No rulers, no math."
    • Band names don't match across three places: Combat prose = Close / Nearby / Far / Remote; Combat table headers = Close / Near / Far / Distant; Zones = Close / Nearby / Far / Distant / Extreme (a fifth band).
    • Zone cross-links are broken: Zones links to combat/#free-fire-opportunities (no such section — and free-fire roll is used but never defined anywhere) and combat/#range-modifiers (the real anchor is #weapon-range-modifiers).
    • Suggested fix: Make Zones the canonical spatial model and reduce Combat → Ranges to a pointer ("Distances are handled as tactical zones — see the Zones chapter"). Then:
    • Delete the meters-based movement line from Combat, or reframe "10 meters" as flavor only.
    • Standardize one set of band labels everywhere (recommend the Zones set: Close / Nearby / Far / Distant / Extreme) and update the Weapon Range Modifiers table headers to match.
    • Define free-fire roll in Combat (it's referenced by Zones → Suppressed zone but never specified), and fix both anchors.
    • Update the psionic talent ranges in Character ("handgun range," "rifle range," "within 10 meters," "within 1 kilometer") to the zone bands, or add a one-line conversion table so "handgun range = Nearby," "rifle range = Far," etc.

    2. Per-file issues¶

    2.1 Operational Preface (index.md)¶

    • "action film" — fine for the 1980s. No period issues. The Preface is the cleanest page.
    • Nav/title note: this page carries the newer chrome; see §0.

    2.2 Operations (playing.md)¶

    • Broken markdown — Action Rolls › Notes: the proper *equipment(), your actions will generally always succeed — stray ( and unterminated italics. Fix: the proper *equipment*, your actions will generally always succeed. (Same note renders \*action roll* with a leading backslash; check the source for an over-escaped asterisk.)
    • Tier label drift: Action Rolls defines 10–12 as success, but Challenges and Danger (and the Appendix) call it complete success. Pick one term and use it everywhere (recommend complete success, since it pairs cleanly with partial and critical).
    • Clock progress vs tier names: Challenges and Danger says "for a complete success, check off 2 segments." Confirm the clock uses the same four tier names as Action Rolls once 2.2's term is standardized.
    • Momentum reset is ambiguous: "momentum… persist between sessions" but "reset to their starting value between missions or whenever you heal or rest." This implies resting lowers a high momentum total back to 2 mid-mission. If intended, say so ("resting resets momentum to its starting value, even if you currently have more"); if not, restrict the reset to between missions only.
    • Reaction Roll (2 / 3–5 / 6–8 / 9–11 / 12) is internally consistent and correctly referenced by the Empathy talent ("shift the reaction roll one category"). No change.

    2.3 Character (character.md)¶

    • +CREATIVE is not an attribute — Psionic Talents #46 (Mind shield): "Roll +CREATIVE to block a psionic attack." The four attributes are STRENGTH, REFLEXES, TACTICS, WILLPOWER. Fix: Roll **+WILLPOWER** to block a psionic attack. (CREATIVE is a leftover from an earlier attribute scheme — search the source for any other instances.)
    • Tactical supplies count contradicts Equipment: Character says "you generally start each mission with three units"; Equipment says "up to 2 units," and the Equipment loadout example uses 2. Fix: reconcile to one number (the loadout example and Equipment both say 2 — recommend 2) and update Character.
    • Weapons table contradicts the damage-class rules and Equipment:
    • "Antitank missile (+2 DAMAGE)" — but Combat classes RPGs as Heavy = +3, and Equipment lists "Antiarmor missile +3." Fix: +3, and align the name (antitank vs antiarmor).
    • "M18 claymore" / "Handgun" / "Shotgun" (Character) vs "Command-detonated mine" / "Sidearm" / "Combat shotgun" (Equipment). Standardize names. (M18 claymore is the more period-flavorful choice if you want specificity; sidearm/SMG/LMG is the Equipment convention.)
    • Grenade launcher has no damage entry in either table — Equipment notes "damage and effect by grenade type," which is clearer; mirror that note in Character or drop the duplicate table.
    • Two full equipment treatments exist (Character → Inventory / Tactical Supplies / Equipment / Basic Kit / Weapons / Additional Gear vs the standalone Equipment chapter). They disagree on: basic-kit contents (Character bundles handgun, grenades, body armor, helmet into the kit; Equipment's basic kit is sundries only and excludes weapons/armor), tactical-supply handling (mark-off-and-have vs swap-a-unit-for-the-item-which-then-takes-slots), and backpack (Character) vs rucksack (Equipment). Fix: delete the equipment material from Character and link to the Equipment chapter, keeping in Character only the creation-step references it needs.
    • Inventory slot count agrees (12: 2 hands / 4 body / 6 pack) across both — good; keep that.

    2.4 Combat (combat.md)¶

    • Sniper rifle range modifier looks inverted — Weapon Range Modifiers: Sniper Rifle shows disadvantage at Distant (the longest band) and "impossible at Close." A precision long-range weapon taking a penalty at its best range is surprising; confirm intent. If the intent is "no scope picture up close, optimal at long range," the Distant cell should likely be "–" and Close "impossible/disadvantage."
    • Heavy-weapon damage class vs Heavy-weapons skill: "Heavy" as a +3 damage class (RPGs, miniguns, sniper rifles) overlaps confusingly with the Heavy weapons skill (grenade launchers, anti-tank missiles) and the Remote = "Heavy weapons range" band. Consider renaming the damage class (e.g., Devastating) or footnoting that these are three different uses of "heavy."
    • Morale check reads backwards on first pass: "morale rated from 1 (scared rabble) to 6 (trained professional)… roll 1D6; if the result equals or exceeds the morale score, they rout." This is mechanically sound (low morale = low threshold = routs almost always; morale 6 routs only on a 6) but counterintuitive. Fix: add a one-line example — "A morale-2 rabble routs on a 2+ (almost always); a morale-6 professional routs only on a 6."
    • "save" vs "morale check": Morale Checks says lone foes "must save" — switch to "must check morale" for consistency.
    • Ammo rule edge case: "Check off 1 ammo for all attack rolls of 9 or less" means 10+ hits never consume ammo. This is a deliberate, clean design but easy to misread as a typo; a half-line ("clean hits 10+ don't burn ammo") would pre-empt the question.
    • Death rule: see §1.2 (replace with Appendix version).
    • Phrasing consistency: the death save uses "13 or more"; elsewhere it's "13 or greater" (Operations, Stress) and "13 or higher." Pick one across the manual.

    2.5 Zones (zones.md)¶

    • Broken cross-links and undefined term: see §1.8 (#free-fire-opportunities, #range-modifiers, undefined free-fire roll).
    • Diagram labels ≠ zone table: the embedded SVG reads Forest edge / Kill Zone / Loading Dock / Catwalk / Office / Generator Room / Research Facility, but the table below names them Forest edge / Kill Zone / Main floor / Catwalks / Office / Generator / Research facility. "Loading Dock" and "Generator Room" appear only in the diagram. Fix: relabel the SVG to match the table (and the numbered walkthrough, which uses "main floor," "catwalk," "generator," "facility").
    • Five bands vs four elsewhere: the Zone Distances table adds Extreme (4+ hops), a band that doesn't exist in Combat. Fold into the §1.8 band-name reconciliation.
    • Minor wording: "Office — Enclosed with windows and a solid cover" → "solid cover" (drop the article); the Zones Example table's "Office" description otherwise reads cleanly.

    2.6 Equipment (equipment.md)¶

    • Broken cross-links: combat/#combat-rounds (combat rounds live under Actions, no #combat-rounds anchor), combat/#rest-and-recovery (real anchor is #recovery, appears twice), combat/#wounds (combat uses Injuries, #injuries). The combat/#blast-damage and appendix/#national-armament-roster links are correct.
    • "laying-up point" vs "rendezvous point": Equipment (and the Appendix) use laying-up point; Combat/Stress/Character use rendezvous point. Both are real SF terms and period-appropriate, but pick one for the manual. (If you keep laying-up point, update Combat → Recovery, Stress → Reducing Stress, and the talent recharge line in Character.)
    • Helmet auto-unconscious immunity isn't reflected in Combat: Equipment says a helmet means "you are not knocked unconscious when wounded," but Combat's knockout rule (the +STRENGTH-or-unconscious step) never mentions the exception. The Appendix death table does include it — another reason to adopt §1.2.
    • Stray empty table row in Mission-Specific Equipment › Examples (a blank | | | row between Suppressor and Surveillance kit). Delete it.
    • "up to 2 units" of tactical supplies — "up to" invites the question of why you'd take fewer than max. If there's a trade-off (slots), state it; otherwise "you start each mission with 2 units."
    • Squad radio range vs Character basic kit: Equipment gives outdoor "~500 m–3 km"; Character's basic kit lists "Radio, squad (range 1km)." Once Character's equipment block is removed (2.3) this resolves itself; otherwise reconcile.
    • Period flavor is strong here — PRC radio, lensatic compass, grease pencil, ChemLight, det cord, mylar blanket, morphine, nerve-agent antidotes all land. One mild anachronism: IFAK (the acronym) is a 2000s-era US term; the kit existed in the 80s but would more likely read as "individual first-aid kit" or "field dressing pouch." Minor; spell it out or drop the acronym for register.

    2.7 Appendix (appendix.md)¶

    • Two # H1 headings on one page: # Appendix and # Rules Summary. For clean document structure (and TOC/print behavior) demote Rules Summary to ## or split it into its own page. The index's appendix/#rules-summary link still resolves either way.
    • Grammar — Momentum table: "You gain 1 points of momentum" → "1 point."
    • All the four-tier summary tables here are the more polished versions of their body-chapter counterparts (Initiative, Retreating, Wounds, Stress). They're the reason to treat the newer set as canonical (§0). The summary tables should match the body once §1 reconciliations are made — right now the summary contradicts the chapters it summarizes, which is the most reader-damaging state for a rules reference.

    3. Period accuracy — National Armament Roster (Appendix)¶

    The roster is mostly solid for a late-1980s setting. Flags, in priority order:

    • France, Assault rifle: "FAMAS G1" — likely an error. The standard French service rifle of the 1980s is the FAMAS F1 (in service from 1978). The G1 was a later, limited ergonomic variant; the G2 is 1994. Fix: FAMAS F1.
    • Israel, Sidearm: "CZ 75" — questionable. The CZ 75 is Czech and not characteristically Israeli-issue. Period-appropriate IDF options: FN Browning Hi-Power (widely used) or, if you accept 1990, the Jericho 941. Fix: Browning Hi-Power for a clean 80s fit.
    • France, LMG: "Minimi" — France didn't adopt the FN Minimi until ~1999; in the 80s the squad MG was the AA-52. Either drop "Minimi" from the France cell or note it as foreign procurement.
    • UK, Grenade launcher: "HK79" — the HK79 mounts on HK rifles (G3/HK33), not the British L85. Period UK underbarrel options were limited; consider "—" or a rifle-grenade note.
    • Whole-table caveat: several entries are post-1983/85 — M16A2, Beretta M9, AT4, M249, L85A1, L96A1, LAW 80, Panzerfaust 3. All fine for a late-80s campaign; if your setting is early-80s, swap to era-correct predecessors (e.g., M16A1, M1911A1 only, M72 LAW only, L1A1 SLR for the UK). State the campaign's baseline year somewhere so the roster reads as intentional.
    • Soviet/US/West German columns are otherwise clean. Israel's blank sniper cell could be filled with the Galatz (Galil Sniper) if you want symmetry, but a dash is acceptable.

    4. Quick-win checklist (lowest effort, highest clarity)¶

    1. Fix +CREATIVE → +WILLPOWER (Character, Mind shield). (Hard error.)
    2. Fix *equipment(), → *equipment*, (Operations, Action Rolls note). (Hard error.)
    3. Fix the four broken anchors in Zones and Equipment; define free-fire roll. (Broken links.)
    4. Relabel the Zones SVG to match the zone table. (Visible mismatch.)
    5. Delete the stray empty table row in Equipment. (Render bug.)
    6. "1 points" → "1 point"; pick "13 or higher" everywhere. (Copy.)
    7. FAMAS G1 → FAMAS F1; reconsider CZ 75 for Israel. (Period.)
    8. Then tackle §1.1–1.8 as a single reconciliation pass with the Appendix as the source of truth.
